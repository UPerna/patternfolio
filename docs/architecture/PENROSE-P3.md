# Penrose P3 from a regular pentagrid

The family tiling/penrose-p3@1 uses de Bruijn's published pentagrid dual.
Its immutable anchor comprises the world frame, physical edge length,
rational phase and construction version. The projection/multigrid provider
is exercised by this family. P2 kite and dart remain optional.

The grid families have unit directions at multiples of 72 degrees. Each
line has dot(z, u_j) + gamma_j equal to an integer. Phase numerators have a
shared positive denominator and sum to zero. For each triple of distinct
families, a rational and a golden-ratio coefficient residue certify that
three lines cannot meet. Both named phase profiles pass all ten tests.
Singular or uncertified phases are rejected.

At a two-family crossing, the two line indices and three exact ceilings
give the base integer lift. The four rhombus vertices increment zero or
one in the two crossing coordinates. Signed sine ratios are evaluated in
the integer ring generated by the golden ratio. Exact algebraic sign
comparisons establish the ceilings. The displayed positions evaluate the
lift against the five roots of unity. Vertex identity uses the lift and
index, and cell identity uses the crossing address. Translation, rotation
and orientation are explicit. The recorded lineage is the lifted grid
construction and not a substitution ancestry.

Thick rhombi have angles 72 and 108 degrees. Thin rhombi have angles 36
and 144 degrees. Edges carry physical arrow direction and multiplicity.
Green double arrows connect 2 to 1 and 3 to 4. Red single arrows connect
indices 2 and 3 from the acute to the obtuse endpoint. Both incident
rhombi must agree on the color and the actual arrow direction. The
diagnostic style paints those arrows. The clean style uses plain borders.

The source bound places a dual tile near its primal crossing. All four
corners lie within five dual units of the affine image of the crossing.
Every point of the rhombus satisfies the same bound by convexity. The
enumerator uses outward-rounded line index ranges and a second halo to
recover both true neighbors of every selected edge. Queries are bounded
in physical and grid coordinates. The window selector retains original
whole tiles, and the renderer clips paint. A spread uses two views of one
construction, with the same lifted tile IDs on both sides of the gutter.

The acceptance oracle independently intersects primal lines at 76 decimal
digits, checks the modular witnesses, compares finite clipped area and
pairwise overlap, and verifies both physical arrows and cell addresses.
Successive levels expand the query and count actual added rhombi. This
profile has no substitution count recurrence. The finite checks validate
the generated patch. The source's aperiodicity result applies to the
regular, arrow-matched Penrose construction under the published assumptions.

Primary sources: N. G. de Bruijn, Algebraic theory of Penrose's
non-periodic tilings of the plane, Parts I and II, Proceedings A 84(1)
(1981), pages 39-52 and 53-66. Part I sections 2, 4, 5 and 7 supply the
rule. Part II sections 9-10 cover phases and regularity. Both full
parts are available in the TU/e scan and University of Illinois mirror.
