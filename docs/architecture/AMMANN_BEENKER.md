# Ammann–Beenker tetragrid

`tiling/ammann-beenker@1` uses Beenker's 1982 tetragrid, §§2.2–2.3, equations (2.1)–(2.9) and Theorem 2.1. The provider registers the existing projection/multigrid route. It takes one positive edge length, a named phase and a right-handed world frame in centimetres. Harriss (2007), pp. 377–378, gives complementary four-dimensional projection context. [Beenker report](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/files/4269555/253166.pdf) [Harriss paper](https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2007/bridges2007-377.pdf)

Four families of parallel lines have normals at 0°, 45°, 90° and 135°. Two crossing lines fix two components of the lifted address. Exact sine ratios in Q(√2) determine the ceiling for each other component. The four mesh addresses around a crossing give one counterclockwise tile, a square for perpendicular normals and a 45°/135° rhombus otherwise. Lifted integer vertices and ordered endpoint pairs identify tiles and edges in every view. Each vertex carries physical and complementary internal integer coefficients over the shared denominator 2. The cm drawing point follows by scaling the physical projection and adding the frame origin.

`offset` fixes offsets (1, 2, 3, 4)/17. `centered` fixes four half offsets and translates physical coordinates by the lift (1, 1, 1, 1). For these rational profiles, the four triple dependencies would each require one integer line at a nonintegral phase coordinate. The independent check rotates the centered patch's interior disk through 45° using the lifted address map. Its symmetry claim is limited to that centered profile and disk.

`zero-plus` and `zero-minus` start from zero offsets and resolve singular triples and quadruples using the opposite coherent infinitesimal directions ±(1, 2, 3, 4). These are explicit choices of approach to the source's singular tetragrid in §5.2. The exact first-order residual decides which side of a grid boundary a dual mesh uses. Both signs and all six line pairs at the central quadruple are exercised. The diagnostic shows positive ties in green, negative ties in coral, and double ties in purple.

The source's bounded displacement from twice the grid-plane point gives the enumeration range. The finite selection uses a two-edge-unit halo, plus two more units for global neighbor reconstruction. The viewport is bounded to ±64 edge units from the frame origin, with a 1,000-tile maximum per generated selection. A view is confined to its certified world query. Rounded drawing coordinates only select conservative finite query candidates and drive rendering. Address decisions, physical/internal metadata and shared edge ownership are computed from exact integer lifts.

The independent Decimal checker reconstructs crossings from the line normals, validates the lifted cells and seam owners, clips polygons to each query and checks total coverage and positive-area overlap. It confirms that a small explicit perturbation gives the same tie decisions in the exercised horizon. Theorem 4.3 provides the source's mathematical nonperiodicity result. The independent finite checks certify the exercised patches and have a different scope.
